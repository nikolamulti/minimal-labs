# Color Tokens

Our design system uses semantic color tokens to ensure consistency and meaningful use of colors across the application. These tokens are organized into four main categories: Background, Foreground, Border, and Persistent.

## Background Colors

Background colors are used for the various surfaces and containers in our UI. They help establish hierarchy and separate different sections of the interface.

- `background-primary`: The main background color for the application.
- `background-secondary`: Used for secondary or alternate background areas.
- `background-tertiary`: For tertiary level backgrounds, often used in nested components.
- `background-inverse`: Contrasting background color, useful for creating emphasis.

## Foreground Colors

Foreground colors are primarily used for text and icons. They ensure readability and proper contrast against the background colors.

- `foreground-primary`: The main color for body text.
- `foreground-secondary`: Used for less emphasized text, such as captions or helper text.
- `foreground-tertiary`: For the least prominent text, often used for placeholders or disabled states.
- `foreground-inverse`: Contrasting text color, typically used on dark or colored backgrounds.

## Border Colors

Border colors help define boundaries between elements and can be used to group related items or separate distinct sections.

- `border-default`: The standard border color for most UI elements.
- `border-strong`: A more prominent border color for emphasis.
- `border-subtle`: A softer border color for less distinct separations.
- `border-inverse`: Contrasting border color, used on darker backgrounds or for highlighting.

## Persistent Colors

Persistent colors maintain their meaning across different contexts and are often used for conveying specific information or states.

- `persistent-error`: Used consistently to indicate errors or critical issues.
- `persistent-success`: Represents successful actions or positive states.
- `persistent-warning`: Indicates warnings or cautionary information.
- `persistent-info`: Used for informational or neutral messaging.
- `persistent-accent`: The primary accent color of the application, used for key interactive elements.

## Usage

When applying these color tokens in your designs or code, always use the semantic token names rather than specific color values. This ensures consistency and makes it easier to update the color scheme globally if needed.
